Perceptual organization is widely agreed to be a critical step connecting low- and high-level processing stages in the perceptual processing hierarchy. However, the majority of research on the different processes of perceptual organization has been conducted using simplistic stimuli with high parametric control. In this study, we extend this work by creating a large dataset of visual annotations on a body of photographs and digital images of paintings, more closely resembling our visual reality.
During a large online study (N ~ 300) participants annotate images in five perceptual grouping tasks (clustering, segregating, linking, layering and configuring) and three higher-order image characteristics (emphasis, repetition, balance), relating to overall image composition. The study includes 40 photographs and 40 paintings, which are randomly assigned to the participants and task combinations. Each participant will annotate around 15 images for one grouping and one higher order task, using a novel online image annotation tool with a limited set of simple colors, lines, basic shapes, and free-form shapes. We provide clear instructions for each of the tasks, with a useful example. In addition, we will also ask for their aesthetic appreciation and visual expertise.
With these annotations, it will be possible to explore the hierarchy and interplay of different principles of perceptual grouping in rich images with more ecological validity, as well as to investigate their role for the overall composition principles of the images. Through hierarchical analysis of the different regions and annotations, we can explore how the different perceptual organization factors related to one another as well as to low-level image properties and aesthetic qualities.
The result is an unprecedented dataset, and a comprehensive investigation of perceptual grouping, image composition, and aesthetics.
